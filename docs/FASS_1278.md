# The single line of `1278-0100100000` — a FASS-curve dossier

**Claim.** Under edge selection **1278** with matching combination **`0100100000`**
(canonical share string `1278-0100100000`), the drawn strands of the Spectre
tiling organize into a single self-avoiding curve. On the **Psi supertile** the
strand diagram at *every* computed substitution level (1–6) is **exactly one
open arc** — no circuits, no other components — that passes through **every
tile** of the patch. The evidence below supports each letter of **FASS**
(space-**F**illing, self-**A**voiding, **S**imple, **S**elf-similar), with the
computational/argued status of each stated explicitly.

All numbers in this document were computed in this repo by the scripts in
[`web/fass-investigation/`](../web/fass-investigation/README.md) (built solely
on the verified `web/src/core` library), or come from the verified dataset
(`graph_analysis/lvl4.csv` / `lvl6.csv`, `web/public/data/circuit_stats.json`,
see `docs/STATS_NOTES.md`) — and every dataset number used here was *also*
independently re-derived by these scripts.

### 1.1 Which one is "the single line"?

Circuit-freeness is *necessary* for a single line: a circuit is a separate
closed component, and every 1278 combo also has open tails (4 at the Delta
root at every level, 1 at the Psi root — scripts 02/10). So only the quartet
qualifies. Among them (Delta root, lvl6 tail profiles — CSV, re-derived
exactly by script 06):

* `0100100000` → {248348, 108864, 1, 1} — one dominant curve (69.5% of all
  357,214 segments) + secondary + two 1-segment corner stubs;
* `0010100000` → its Theta/Lambda twin, {248132, 109080, 1, 1};
* `0011000000` / `0101000000` → {178463, ~98090, ~80660, 1} — same structure
  phase-shifted by one substitution level (their even-level profile is the
  others' odd-level profile; script 03).

All four are single-line configurations at the Psi root (script 10). The
flagship is **`0100100000`**: it is the one the owner documented (see §2), and
it holds the longest-tail record in the verified dataset.

### 1.2 The Psi story

The owner remembered the combination "involved the Psi tile's setting
especially". Two findings, honestly separated:

1. **Psi's combo digit is structurally forced.** Under 1278 Psi has only two
   active dots (`-1A`, `-2A`), hence exactly one perfect matching; digit 7
   is a decode no-op (script 09: `0100100100`, `0100100200`, `0100100500` all
   decode to the same configuration). *No 1278 combo varies Psi.* So the
   memory cannot refer to a choice made at the Psi digit — plausibly it was
   Pi (digit 4, which *is* set to 1 in the winner and whose choice
   `-1A—-2A` copies Psi's forced diagonal), or:
2. **The Psi *supertile* is the single line.** Under any circuit-free combo,
   the level-k Psi supertile's strand diagram is ONE open arc and nothing
   else, at every computed level (see §4). If "the Psi tile's setting" meant
   "look at the Psi supertile", the memory is exactly right — Psi is where
   the whole FASS structure lives.

## 4. The FASS properties

### 4.1 Simple (no branch points, components are disjoint simple curves) — PROVED

1278 has no class-0 edge, so every dot is a two-tile seam crossing: welded
degree ≤ 2 (structural). Verified computationally in every run:
`junctionCount = 0` and max welded-point degree = 2 (Delta@1–6, Psi@1–6, all
9 supertile types at levels 1–5). Distinct arcs share no point (a shared
point would need degree ≥ 2 with tails ending there; the tracer walks through
degree-2 points, so tails end only at degree-1 boundary dots).

### 4.2 Self-avoiding (no geometric crossings) — PROVED at k ≤ 5

Script 05 checked **every** bbox-close pair of welded segments (spatial hash,
orientation predicates with ε = 1e-9 against fp noise ~1e-12):

| patch | segments | pairs checked | proper crossings | T-touches | collinear overlaps | min clearance |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Delta@3 | 733 | 1,398 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0.607 |
| Delta@4 | 5,764 | 11,043 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0.607 |
| Delta@5 | 45,373 | 87,090 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0.607 |
| Psi@4 | 5,761 | 11,042 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0.607 |

Min clearance 0.607 **world units** (tile edge = 1) between non-adjacent
features — six orders of magnitude above numerical noise, so the epsilon
verdicts are unambiguous. Non-crossing at higher levels follows from
self-similarity (§4.4): the set of local two-chord configurations inside a
tile and across a seam is finite and identical at every level.

### 4.3 Space-filling (visits every tile) — PROVED at k ≤ 6; limit argued

Every tile type carries ≥ 1 chord under 1278 (1 chord for
Delta/Sigma/Phi/Psi/Gamma2, 2 for Theta/Lambda/Xi/Pi/Gamma1), so every tile is
crossed by some strand. Script 06 verified the single Psi-root arc actually
*is* all of it:

| level | tiles (Psi patch) | segments | arcs | longest arc | share | tiles visited by the arc | segs/tile |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 9 | 10 | **1** | 10 | 100% | 9 = all | 1.1111 |
| 2 | 71 | 91 | **1** | 91 | 100% | 71 = all | 1.2817 |
| 3 | 559 | 730 | **1** | 730 | 100% | 559 = all | 1.3059 |
| 4 | 4,401 | 5,761 | **1** | 5,761 | 100% | 4,401 = all | 1.3090 |
| 5 | 34,649 | 45,370 | **1** | 45,370 | 100% | 34,649 = all | 1.3094 |
| 6 | 272,791 | 357,211 | **1** | 357,211 | 100% | 272,791 = all | 1.3095 |

(Delta root for comparison: 4 arcs; at even levels — where the profile is
{big, mid, 1, 1} — the two big arcs together visit every tile (levels 2, 4, 6
computed); at odd levels the three substantial arcs share the job. The
dominant arc alone visits ~70% of tiles at even levels.)

Tile count multiplies by 7.87298 per level (34649/4401 = 272791/34649 =
7.872983…), matching **4 + √15 = 7.8729833…** (the substitution's Perron
eigenvalue) to 6 significant digits; segments/tile converges to ≈ 1.3095.
The curve's length therefore scales with the patch *area*: rescaling Psi@k to
constant diameter, the arc comes within O(λ^(−k/2)) of every point (each tile
of the patch is visited and tiles shrink uniformly). That is the standard
space-filling limit statement; the finite-k facts backing it are proved,
the Hausdorff-limit formalization is argued, not machine-checked.

### 4.4 Self-similar — VERIFIED for k ≤ 5 (all 9 supertile types); induction argued

Method (script 04): for each supertile type T at level k, weld the whole
patch, then (a) trace each child sub-supertile alone → the child's **routing**
= pairing of its boundary dots by its internal arcs; (b) record which child
dots weld to which sibling dots (**gluing**) and which become the parent's
boundary dots (**outer**); (c) record each parent arc as its exact sequence of
child arcs (**composition**). Boundary dots are labelled canonically along the
child's outline polygon (edge-cancellation outline, anchored at the
supertile's quad corner with a chirality-stable direction rule, so labels
survive the per-level mirror flip of `buildSupertiles`).

Computed for all 9 types × levels 1–5 (combo `0100100000`; the other three
circuit-free combos were also run):

* **No circuits, no junctions in any supertile type at any level 1–5.**
* Interface sizes are constant from level 1: Gamma 10, Delta 8, Theta 6,
  Lambda 6, Xi 4, Pi 4, Sigma 10, Phi 4, **Psi 2** boundary dots.
* **Psi is a single arc `0-1`** — one curve from one boundary dot to the
  other — at every level (segment counts 10, 91, 730, 5761, 45370; 357,211 at
  level 6 via script 06). Its two endpoints sit on the Gamma child
  (`outer: 0←7:9, 1←7:8`) at every level.
* **Routing stabilizes at k₀ = 3 with period 2**: `signature(T,3) =
  signature(T,5)` for all 9 types — signatures include the child pairings,
  gluing, outer map, *and the full composition sequences*. Adjacent levels
  differ (the substitution mirror-flips each level; the boundary pairing of
  Delta alternates between `{0-7,1-6,2-3,4-5}` and its one-step rotation
  `{0-1,2-7,3-4,5-6}`). For `0010100000`/`0101000000`, `signature(T,2) =
  signature(T,4)` holds as well.
* **The composition operator is level-independent**: gluing + outer maps are
  identical for every level 2–5, for all 9 types, in all 4 circuit-free
  combos. Example, the Psi rule (fixed sequence threading its children
  0=Psi, 1=Delta, 2=Psi, 3=Phi, 4=Sigma, 5=Psi, 6=Phi, 7=Gamma; `p:a-b` means
  "the child at position p, its arc joining its boundary dots a,b"):

  ```text
  Psi@even: 7:0-9 > 0:0-1 > 1:0-1 > 7:1-2 > 1:2-7 > 2:0-1 > 3:0-1 > 1:3-4 > 3:2-3 > 4:0-1 > 1:5-6
            > 4:8-9 > 7:3-4 > 4:2-7 > 5:0-1 > 6:0-1 > 4:3-4 > 6:2-3 > 7:6-7 > 4:5-6 > 7:5-8
  Psi@odd:  7:4-9 > 4:7-8 > 7:2-3 > 1:0-7 > 7:0-1 > 0:0-1 > 1:1-6 > 4:0-9 > 1:4-5 > 3:0-3 > 1:2-3
            > 2:0-1 > 3:1-2 > 4:1-6 > 7:5-6 > 4:4-5 > 6:0-3 > 4:2-3 > 5:0-1 > 6:1-2 > 7:7-8
  ```

  Note the single parent arc consumes **every arc of every child exactly
  once** (21 visits: the children have 1+4+1+2+5+1+2+5 arcs at even levels) —
  the Hilbert-style "substitute and keep all the paths" rule from the README,
  made explicit.
* **Consistency**: every child interface pairing equals the standalone
  routing of that child type at that level, arc-by-arc including segment
  counts.

**Induction argument** (argued, not machine-checked beyond k = 5): routing at
level k+1 is a fixed function F_T (determined by gluing + outer) of the
children's routings at level k. Gluing/outer were verified identical at every
computed level, and structurally they are pinned by the level-independent
child transforms of `buildSupertiles` plus the constant interface counts.
Given `routing(·,3) = routing(·,5)`, applying the same F repeatedly gives
`routing(·,k) = routing(·,k+2)` for all k ≥ 3 — the curve is generated by a
fixed (period-2, because of the mirror) substitution rule at every scale.
This is exactly self-similarity in the FASS sense.

### 4.5 The tails, honestly — boundary-anchored limit

A FASS curve is an infinite-limit object; finite patches must have endpoints.
Script 03 (levels 1–5, tolerance 1e-6 against the exact patch outline):

* Delta root: always exactly 4 arcs, and **all 8 endpoints lie on the patch
  boundary** at every level. The two 1-segment stubs are corner tiles whose
  both dots are boundary dots — pure edge-of-patch artifacts.
* Psi root: always exactly 1 arc; both endpoints on the boundary.

Merge analysis (script 04, nested Delta@k ⊂ Delta@(k+1) at child position 1,
verified for child levels k = 1…4 inside parent levels 2…5): at odd→even steps **all four** child arcs (dominant,
secondary, both stubs) merge into the parent's single dominant arc; at
even→odd steps the child's dominant + both stubs merge into one parent arc
and the secondary into another. So over any two consecutive levels,
*everything* a patch contains lands in a single arc two levels up: in the
nested-union limit all strands belong to **one** bi-infinite curve. The
nested-Psi limit (Psi is child 0 of Psi) is even simpler: one arc inside one
arc at every level. The finite-patch "4 tails" are not four curves — they are
four boundary-cut windows onto the same line.

   maps are level-independent for *all* k (currently verified k = 2–5), e.g.
   by proving the child-boundary dot positions near each weld are eventually
   periodic in k under the substitution.
2. **Hull-wide statement**: the merge analysis covers the canonical nested
   sequences (Delta-in-Delta at position 1; Psi-in-Psi at position 0). Other
   infinite tilings in the substitution hull correspond to other nesting
   addresses; classify whether any address yields two bi-infinite curves
   instead of one (the even→odd step does split dominant/secondary before the
   next step re-merges them).
3. **Closed forms**: segments/tile → ≈ 1.30949 and the tile growth factor
   4 + √15; derive both exactly from the substitution matrix.
4. **The other three combos**: `0010100000` (Theta/Lambda twin) and the
   phase-shifted pair `0011000000`/`0101000000` have the same Psi-root
   single-line property; their limit curves' mutual relation (same curve up
   to mirror? genuinely different FASS curves?) is unexplored.
5. Whether the two big Delta-root arcs converge to the *same* bi-infinite
   curve in every hull tiling would decide the Explainer's "one line
   threading the entire aperiodic floor" question in full generality.
